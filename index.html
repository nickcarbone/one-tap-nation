Today · The Story

The app that swallowed a country

In most of the world, the most important app on a phone isn't one an American has heard of. It started as one thing: a call centre for motorbike taxis, a text message that moved money, a messenger built after an earthquake. Then it became the way a country moves, eats and pays. Its drivers, couriers and cash agents became a visible class of worker, and its outages became national emergencies.

This guide picks one per country. The test: the app locals would say you can't function without, backed by penetration data. Regional giants such as Grab, Rappi, Careem and Yandex Go are filed under the country where they were born, with their wider reach noted.

Why so much Asia?Super-apps are largely an Asian invention, and the list reflects that: 9 of the 20 sit in our Asia filter, plus 2 more in the Middle East. The rest span Europe, Africa and Latin America.
Read the fine printEvery entry has a red "fine print" card covering labour, outages, data and state power. Russia and Iran also carry an amber context note on the authoritarian systems their apps operate within.
About the numbersFigures are the latest we could verify as of 18 September 2026 and are mostly company-reported. "Users" means different things to different firms (registered, monthly active, 90-day active), so each card says which.

How we chose

One app per country, chosen because locals treat it as indispensable and adoption data backs that up. WeChat is included as the benchmark every other super-app chased, the lone exception to the "unknown abroad" rule.

What we checked

Histories, figures and controversies were checked against company filings, regulators, and reporting from outlets including Reuters, Bloomberg, Meduza, Rest of World, The Korea Herald, Tuoi Tre and TechCabal. Sources are listed on every card. Ongoing stories (the Grab–GoTo talks, GCash's IPO, Iran's connectivity) may have moved since publication.

A note on design

Icons are invented glyphs, not the apps' real logos, and colours are not brand colours. No trademarks are reproduced. Built as a single self-contained page; it works offline.
